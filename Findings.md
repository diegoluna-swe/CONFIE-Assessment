# Findings

After comparing the AI evaluations against the human evaluations, I found that the model was able to follow the rubric and output structure consistently, but there were some differences in how certain criteria were interpreted.
Most of the disagreements were around **C2 (Needs Discovery)** and **C3 (Compliance)**. In some cases, the model gave a 10 where the human evaluation gave a 5, while in others there were larger differences in scoring. However, after checking the files by hand, I found mixed results. Some of the evaluations from the human side were in the wrong according to my point of view, either the first message being mangled by the urgency of the situation, some idioms from the agents that aren't technically wrong but show very little professionalism, simple straight questions that might seem rude but are actually perfect. I must say that the model I chose, for not having any kind of specialized training (that I know of), and being on the lower end, it had very good results.
Overall, the prototype worked as expected, but the testing showed that having more explicit instructions and mainly, more examples for the model, could help reduce these interpretation differences and make the evaluations more consistent.
